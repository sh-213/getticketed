How Many Questions Are on the CSCS Test?

The CITB Health, Safety & Environment (HS&E) test — the test you sit to get most CSCS cards — has 50 multiple-choice questions and a 45-minute time limit. That applies whether you're sitting the Operatives version or the Managers & Professionals version. (Source: CITB — Operatives test and About the HS&E test.)

The pass mark

You need at least 45 of the 50 questions correct to pass — that's 90%. There's no partial credit for "close enough" topics and no separate pass mark per section; it's one combined score out of 50. (Source: CITB — Operatives test.)

It's not split evenly across topics

The Managers & Professionals version of the test is officially built from five sections: Legal and management, Occupational health, General safety, High risk activities, and Environment. The Operatives test draws from the same broad range of construction health and safety topics — PPE, manual handling, working at height, fire safety, hazardous substances, signs and signals, accident reporting, and general site safety — but CITB doesn't publish a fixed number of questions per topic, so you can't predict exactly how many questions you'll get on any one subject. (Source: CITB — About the HS&E test.)

Three test versions, same format

CITB runs three versions of the HS&E test depending on your role: Operatives (general site workers), Specialists (trade-specific roles), and Managers & Professionals (management positions). All three use the same 50-question, 45-minute, 90%-pass-mark format — the difference is the question content, not the structure. (Source: CITB — Health, safety and environment test.)

What the official test costs

As of CITB's current published pricing, the standard HS&E test costs £23.50 at a Pearson Professional Centre. Mobile testing events (where CITB brings testing to a site or local venue) cost more — £32.75 per test. (Source: CITB official pricing documentation, April 2026.) Prices can change, so always check CITB's own booking page for the current fee before you book.
